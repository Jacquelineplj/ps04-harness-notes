# The Harness Grew Up: What Changed in Agent Engineering This Month

Runtime target: about 4 minutes 15 seconds (~620 narration words)

## 1. Hook

**Visual:** Dark slide, large white text. Beneath it, two arrows pointing in opposite directions labelled "Vendors: into the model" and "Research: into the environment".

**On-screen text:** Where does your agent's state actually live?

**Narration:** This month's agent-engineering news looks scattered, but it all answers one question: where does the state a running agent depends on actually live, and who's accountable for it? Vendors answered by moving orchestration into the model. Measured research answered by grading the environment outside it. We think the research is the part you should act on first — and here's why.

## 2. Anthropic's dynamic workflows

**Visual:** Quote card — "Latent Space, 12 October 2026". Below, a simple diagram: a lead agent box writing a program that spawns three subagent boxes sharing one filesystem icon.

**On-screen text:** Lead agent writes its own orchestration

**Narration:** Anthropic announced "dynamic workflows." Instead of a developer configuring a coordinator and a roster of subagents, the lead agent writes its own orchestration program — launching agents, collecting results, managing information flow. [01-c1] This came out of a presentation with audience Q&A, not a controlled study, so treat it as an announcement.

## 3. The presenters' own caveats

**Visual:** Quote card with two pull-quotes attributed to Anthropic presenters Stall and Avilla, source line "Latent Space, 12 October 2026".

**On-screen text:** "much more" cost — presenters' words

**Narration:** The sharpest caveats came from the presenters themselves. Stall said the model does not know its budget while it's running. And in their watchlist-scanner comparison, Avilla said flows cost "much more" than a single-agent approach — while also being "much more effective at finding all of the details." [01-c2] That's a verbal characterisation, not a published number.

## 4. Microsoft's opposite bet

**Visual:** Split slide. Left: "A2A — 6, 6, 7 model calls." Right: "Skills — 3, 3, 3 model calls." Footer: "Microsoft, 16 September 2026 — author's own trace comparison."

**On-screen text:** Skills in context, not a separate loop

**Narration:** Microsoft published a structurally opposed pattern: rather than spawning a specialist agent loop, specialist instructions load into the orchestrator's context as distributed skills over MCP. [01-c3] The author's own three-pair trace comparison showed three model calls for the skills path versus six or seven for A2A — and the post explicitly disclaims being a controlled performance or quality study. [01-c3] Microsoft also shipped a Foundry hosted-agent isolation model separating user isolation — whose data may be used — from session isolation — where code and files persist. Its pointed note: a hosted session is not a conversation. A conversation holds message history; a session holds sandbox compute and persisted files. [01-c9]

## 5. The number that should worry you

**Visual:** Large stat card: "67.24%". Subtitle: "of failures looked clean." Source line: "ThinkingBox benchmark, 3 October 2026."

**On-screen text:** Two-thirds of failures look like success

**Narration:** Now the measured side. The ThinkingBox benchmark grades agents on terminal backend database state, not on what the agent said. Across 121,680 valid trials, 79,853 attempts failed the executable checks — and 67.24% of those failures still terminated cleanly, invoked a state-changing tool, and reported no final tool error. [01-c4] If you monitor final-message success and tool-error rates, that's roughly two-thirds of your failures invisible.

## 6. Reliability results worth acting on

**Visual:** Three stacked result bars: "Fault selection 86.2% → 2.9%", "Snapshots 4/5 vs git-diff 0/5", "Cost $11.85 → $8.34". Each with its source and date underneath.

**On-screen text:** Three measured wins, three single studies

**Narration:** Three concrete results. RATED reports that reranking tool retrieval with a contextual bandit updated from live execution feedback cuts fault selection rate from 86.2% to 2.9% under a 30% injected failure rate, at under one millisecond of added latency. [01-c5] A sandbox-snapshot paper reports that full-machine restoration beats source-only restoration only when progress lives outside the repository — on a task whose state sat in a SQLite database, snapshots solved four of five attempts while git-diff and clean restarts solved zero of five. [01-c6] And Arize reports that changing a single tool description cut Codex's unnecessary skill loads from fifty-one to zero and lowered a benchmark run's cost from $11.85 to $8.34, with correctness unchanged. [01-c7]

## 7. The thread

**Visual:** Centre node "Where does state live?" with five spokes: shared filesystem, relocated instructions, execution feedback, terminal DB state, snapshots. Corner label: "Our interpretation."

**On-screen text:** Interpretation, not a finding

**Narration:** We read these as one argument, though no source frames it this way: the design question underneath all of them is where state lives and who owns it. Vendors answer with shared filesystems and relocated instructions; the measured work answers with execution feedback, terminal database state, and environment snapshots. [01-c8] That's our interpretation, not a finding. But it suggests that if you're treating context management as prompt assembly, you're solving the wrong layer.

## 8. Limits and takeaway

**Visual:** Two-column slide — "Measured" versus "Announced" — then a closing card with the article title and "Sources linked in description."

**On-screen text:** Measured vs announced — know which is which

**Narration:** Be honest about the limits. The orchestration news is entirely vendor-sourced with no independent measurement; Microsoft's comparison is three request pairs on one demo. [01-c3] Anthropic's cost claim is a presenter's verbal characterisation. [01-c2] And each research result is a single study: ThinkingBox's tasks are synthetic reconstruction, the snapshot headline rests on one custom task at four-of-five versus zero-of-five, [01-c6] RATED's drop is measured under injected outages rather than observed production failures, [01-c5] and Arize's saving is one run. [01-c7] So: treat observable terminal state, not the final message, as your unit of evaluation, and assume your success metrics overstate reliability. [01-c4] The cheapest wins this month were unglamorous and measured. The orchestration shift is, so far, announced. Full article and every source are linked below.
