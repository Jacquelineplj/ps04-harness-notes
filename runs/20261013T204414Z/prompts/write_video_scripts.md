# Video script instructions

Turn the same editorial brief into a 3 to 5 minute narrated video script (450 to 750
narration words) suitable for Google Vids or HeyGen with an optional avatar presenter.
The script must make the same factual claims as the article and cite the same claim
ids; it may drop secondary claims but must not add new ones.

Format (Markdown):
- `# <title>` then a one-line `Runtime target:` estimate.
- Six to nine numbered scenes. Each scene has exactly these labelled lines:
  - `Visual:` what is on screen (slide text, a chart described in words, a quote card
    naming the source, b-roll suggestion). Keep it producible without custom footage.
  - `On-screen text:` at most twelve words.
  - `Narration:` spoken lines in plain conversational English, with claim markers like
    `[01-c2]` after the sentence they support. Markers are not read aloud.
- Scene 1 is the hook and the argument in one breath. The last scene gives the
  takeaway, names the limitation honestly, and points viewers to the article and the
  source list in the description.
- When quoting audio or video evidence, name the show and the rough time, for example
  "on Practical AI, about twelve minutes in". Quote cards should show the publisher
  and date.
- Say "reports", "claims", "we think" so viewers hear the difference between fact,
  source claim, and interpretation. Do not dramatise uncertainty away.
- End with a line `Description:` followed by a two-sentence description for YouTube
  that mentions the article and that sources are linked below (the link list is
  generated automatically).
