# Article writing instructions

Turn the editorial brief into a Substack article for the audience in the project
brief. Write in the publication's voice: direct, concrete, skeptical, no hype.

Format and length:
- Markdown. A title line (`# ...`), then 600 to 900 words of body; aim for about 800
  words so that the final draft stays inside the range (the Sources section is appended
  automatically and does not count).
- Open with a two-sentence stance that states the argument. Use short sections with
  plain headings. Close with a short "What this means for builders" paragraph (piece
  03 closes with "What to watch").
- Cite with claim markers in square brackets immediately after the sentence they
  support, for example `... reduced tool errors by a third [02-c3].` Use only the
  claim ids in the brief; use every claim at least once; never cite a claim for a
  statement it does not support.

Substance:
- Every substantive factual statement carries a marker. Statements without a marker
  are limited to framing, transitions, and clearly labelled interpretation.
- Make the kind of statement visible in the prose: "X reports", "X claims", "we
  read this as", "it is unclear whether". Do not present a source claim as a fact.
- Name publishers and dates for key evidence in the text so readers can judge
  recency and incentive (for example "in a 9 October post", "on the Latent Space
  podcast").
- Include the limitation or counterargument from the brief as a real section, not a
  disclaimer.
- Do not add facts, numbers, names, or quotations that are not in the brief or its
  evidence passages.
- Audio and video evidence should be introduced as such ("said on the podcast at
  12:40") so viewers can find the passage; exact links are added automatically.
